The observed superdiffusion ($\alpha \approx 1.8$) is likely a manifestation of the "frozen-in" nature of large-scale turbulent structures being advected by the background flow, rather than intrinsic vortex dynamics. I hypothesize that the vortex trajectory is not an independent random walk, but is slaved to the local Lagrangian velocity field of the fluid. Specifically, I propose that the vortex displacement $\Delta r$ is highly correlated with the local fluid velocity $u(r, t)$ at the vortex centroid. By decomposing the vortex trajectory into a "background advection" component (the local fluid velocity) and a "residual drift" component (the vortex's motion relative to the local fluid), we can isolate the true diffusive nature of the vortex. I predict that the residual drift will exhibit a significantly lower diffusion exponent ($\alpha_{residual} \approx 1.0 - 1.2$), confirming that the superdiffusion is an artifact of large-scale flow advection rather than Lévy-flight dynamics. This will be tested by computing the Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation of the fluid at the vortex centroids and comparing the MSD of the raw trajectories versus the "advection-subtracted" trajectories.
