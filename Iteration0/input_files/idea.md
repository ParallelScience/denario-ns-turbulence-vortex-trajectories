**Title: Spatial Modulation of Flow Topology by Large-Scale Solenoidal Driving**

This study quantifies how the large-scale solenoidal driving ($n=2$) modulates the small-scale dissipation topology in the 128³ isothermal dataset. We compute the velocity gradient tensor $\nabla \mathbf{v}$ using a fourth-order central difference scheme, applying a low-pass Gaussian filter to the velocity fields to mitigate grid-scale numerical noise from the HLLE/PLM scheme. To account for compressibility, we decompose the tensor into its trace-free (incompressible) and dilatational components, evaluating the joint PDF of the modified invariants ($Q^*, R^*$) conditioned on the local filtered vorticity magnitude and distance from the driving scale. By mapping the spatial correlation between the driving-scale energy injection zones and the local residence time in the "teardrop" regions of the $Q^*-R^*$ plane, we will measure how solenoidal forcing biases the structural alignment of the strain-rate tensor. The analysis will specifically target the suppression of dilatational effects in the vicinity of strong vortex filaments, providing a high-fidelity assessment of how large-scale forcing sustains the cascade against isothermal dissipation. This approach provides a rigorous, scale-dependent characterization of turbulence that respects both the simulation's resolution limits and its specific driving physics.
