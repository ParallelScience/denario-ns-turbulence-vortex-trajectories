The observed superdiffusive regime ($\alpha \approx 1.86$) and the decorrelation time ($\tau_c \approx 0.22$) are fundamentally linked to the topological evolution of the vortex filaments, specifically the "vortex reconnection" events that occur during mergers and splits. I hypothesize that the transition from ballistic-like motion to Brownian diffusion is triggered by the discrete topological reconfiguration of the vortex core, rather than continuous fluid advection. By tracking the "topological age" of a vortex (time since last merge/split event) and correlating it with the local Lagrangian velocity autocorrelation function, we can demonstrate that the decorrelation time $\tau_c$ is a direct function of the vortex lifetime. Specifically, I propose that vortices exhibit ballistic motion ($\alpha \to 2$) during their stable lifetime and undergo a "diffusive reset" during reconnection events. This can be tested by comparing the MSD of "stable" trajectories (those without merge/split events) against the ensemble MSD, expecting the stable subset to maintain a higher $\alpha$ for longer durations, thereby proving that the observed superdiffusion is an ensemble average of ballistic segments interrupted by topological reconfigurations.
